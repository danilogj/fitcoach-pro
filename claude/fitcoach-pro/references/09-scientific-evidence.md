# Scientific Evidence Base & Reference Compendium

> **Keywords:** scientific literature, meta-analysis, systematic review, citations, evidence hierarchy, Schoenfeld, Helms, Phillips, Wolf, Refalo, Robinson, Maeo, Trommelen, Peos, Schumann, Gabbett.

This document compiles the peer-reviewed evidence base underpinning the FitCoach Pro prescription doctrine. Every claim, volume landmark, proximity-to-failure threshold, biomechanical substitution, and nutritional calculation maps to these empirical findings.

## 1. Proximity to Failure, RIR, and Effort Intensity

* **Robinson ZP, Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Refalo MC, Jukic I, Steele J, Zourdos MC.** *Exploring the Dose-Response Relationship Between Estimated Resistance Training Proximity to Failure, Strength Gain, and Muscle Hypertrophy: A Series of Meta-Regressions.* **Sports Med.** 2024 Sep;54(9):2209-2231. [PMID: 38970765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/)
  * *Key Finding:* Proximity to failure has a distinct dose-response relationship with hypertrophy versus strength. Maximal strength gains occur across a wide range of RIR (1–4), whereas hypertrophy exhibits a continuous negative relationship with RIR (higher hypertrophy per set closer to failure). However, non-linear fatigue accumulation makes RIR 1–2 optimal for high-cost compound lifts, while RIR 0–1 or momentary failure is viable for low-cost isolations.

* **Refalo MC, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Hamilton DL, Fyfe JJ.** *Influence of Resistance Training Proximity-to-Failure on Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis.* **Sports Med.** 2023 Mar;53(3):649-665. [PMID: 36334240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/)
  * *Key Finding:* Momentary muscular failure is not superior to non-failure training (RIR 1–2) for muscle hypertrophy when volume load is equated. High velocity loss thresholds and failure-only protocols disproportionately escalate neuromuscular fatigue without additional muscle growth.

* **Refalo MC, Helms ER, Robinson ZP, Hamilton DL, Fyfe JJ.** *Similar muscle hypertrophy following eight weeks of resistance training to momentary muscular failure or with repetitions-in-reserve in resistance-trained individuals.* **J Sports Sci.** 2024 Jan;42(6):529-539. [PMID: 38393985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/)
  * *Key Finding:* In resistance-trained lifters, 2-RIR on leg press and 1-RIR on leg extension produced identical quadriceps hypertrophy to training to momentary failure over 8 weeks, with significantly less repetition and lifting velocity loss across sets.

* **Zourdos MC, Klemp A, Dolan C, Quiles JM, Schau KA, Jo E, Helms E, Esgro B, Duncan S, Garcia Merino S, Blanco R.** *Novel Resistance Training-Specific Rating of Perceived Exertion Scale Measuring Repetitions in Reserve.* **J Strength Cond Res.** 2016 Jan;30(1):267-275. [PMID: 26492102](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26492102/)
  * *Key Finding:* Validated the inverse relationship between movement velocity, force output, and subjective RIR ratings across multi-joint compound lifts, providing the empirical foundation for double progression.

## 2. Muscle Lengths, Biomechanics & Stretch-Mediated Hypertrophy

* **Wolf M, Korakakis PA, Roberts MD, Plotkin DL, Franchi MV, Contreras B, Henselmans M, Larsen S, Schoenfeld BJ.** *Does longer-muscle length resistance training cause greater longitudinal growth in humans? A systematic review.* **Sports Med Health Sci.** 2026 Jan. [PMID: 41646176](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41646176/)
  * *Key Finding:* Resistance training performed at longer muscle lengths (LML) promotes greater longitudinal growth and total hypertrophy than training at shorter muscle lengths (SML), particularly in biarticular muscles susceptible to stretch-mediated passive tension (titin filament strain).

* **Maeo S, Wu Y, Huang M, Sakurai H, Kusagawa Y, Sugiyama T, Kanehisa H, Isaka T.** *Triceps brachii hypertrophy is substantially greater after elbow extension training performed in the overhead versus neutral arm position.* **Eur J Sport Sci.** 2023 Jul;23(7):1240-1250. [PMID: 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)
  * *Key Finding:* Overhead elbow extensions elicited 1.5-fold greater hypertrophy in the long head of the triceps brachii (+28.5% vs +19.6%, $p < 0.001$) and 1.4-fold greater whole triceps growth compared to neutral-arm pushdowns, confirming the regional advantage of long-muscle-length training.

* **Maeo S, Huang M, Wu Y, Sakurai H, Kusagawa Y, Sugiyama T, Kanehisa H, Isaka T.** *Greater Hamstrings Muscle Hypertrophy but Similar Damage Protection after Training at Long versus Short Muscle Lengths.* **Med Sci Sports Exerc.** 2021 Apr 1;53(4):825-837. [PMID: 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/)
  * *Key Finding:* Seated leg curl (hip flexed, lengthening biarticular hamstrings) produced significantly greater volume growth (+14% vs +9%, $p < 0.05$) than prone leg curl across all biarticular heads while conferring equivalent protection against muscle damage.

* **Wolf M, Androulakis Korakakis P, Piñero A, Mohan AE, Hermann T, Augustin F, Sapuppo M, Lin B, Coleman M, Burke R, Nippard J, Swinton PA, Schoenfeld BJ.** *Lengthened partial repetitions elicit similar muscular adaptations as full range of motion repetitions during resistance training in trained individuals.* **PeerJ.** 2025;13:e18904. [PMID: 39959841](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39959841/)
  * *Key Finding:* Training exclusively in lengthened partials yields comparable muscular adaptations to full ROM in trained lifters. Emphasizing the deep stretched position drives optimal hypertrophy.

* **Larsen S, Sandberg NØ, Schoenfeld BJ, Fredriksen AB, Kristiansen BS, Wolf M, van den Tillaar R, Swinton PA, Falch HN.** *Resistance Training Beyond Momentary Failure: The Effects of Past-Failure Partials Versus Initial Partials on Calf Muscle Hypertrophy Among a Resistance-Trained Cohort.* **Eur J Sport Sci.** 2025 Sep. [PMID: 40850937](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40850937/)
  * *Key Finding:* Extending sets beyond momentary failure using lengthened partial repetitions ("past-failure partials") represents a potent intensification technique for stubborn distal muscles without requiring additional warm-up.

* **Kassiano W, Costa B, Nunes JP, Ribeiro AS, Schoenfeld BJ, Cyrino ES.** *Which ROMs Lead to Rome? A Systematic Review of the Effects of Range of Motion on Muscle Hypertrophy.* **J Strength Cond Res.** 2023 May 1;37(5):1135-1144. [PMID: 36662126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36662126/)
  * *Key Finding:* Full ROM and initial partial ROM (lengthened phase) elicited superior hypertrophy of the rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, biceps brachii, and gluteal musculature compared to partial ROM in the shortened position.

* **Pallarés JG, Hernández-Belmonte A, Martínez-Cava A, Vetrovsky T, Steffl M, Courel-Ibáñez J.** *Effects of range of motion on resistance training adaptations: A systematic review and meta-analysis.* **Scand J Med Sci Sports.** 2021 Oct;31(10):1866-1881. [PMID: 34170576](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34170576/)
  * *Key Finding:* Full ROM training produces significantly greater lower-limb hypertrophy (ES = 0.88) and maximal strength gains (ES = 0.56) than partial ROM in the shortened position.

## 3. Weekly Set Volume, MEV/MRV & Dose-Response

* **McLeod JC, Currier BS, Lowisz CV, Phillips SM.** *The influence of resistance exercise training prescription variables on skeletal muscle mass, strength, and physical function in healthy adults: An umbrella review.* **J Sport Health Sci.** 2024 Jan;13(1):15-28. [PMID: 37385345](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37385345/)
  * *Key Finding:* Umbrella review of 44 systematic reviews confirming that weekly set volume is the primary driver of hypertrophy. Set volume within 6–30 reps dictates hypertrophic adaptations.

* **Baz-Valle E, Balsalobre-Fernández C, Alix-Fages C, Santos-Concejero J.** *A Systematic Review of The Effects of Different Resistance Training Volumes on Muscle Hypertrophy.* **J Hum Kinet.** 2022 Jan;81:199-210. [PMID: 35291645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35291645/)
  * *Key Finding:* Confirms that a range of 12–20 weekly sets per muscle group represents the optimum standard recommendation for trained individuals, with high-volume thresholds (>20 sets) providing selective advantage in stubborn muscle groups (e.g. triceps).

* **Androulakis-Korakakis P, Fisher JP, Steele J.** *The Minimum Effective Training Dose Required to Increase 1RM Strength in Resistance-Trained Men: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* **Sports Med.** 2020 Apr;50(4):751-765. [PMID: 31797219](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31797219/)
  * *Key Finding:* A single set performed 2–3 times per week at high effort intensity (RIR 1–2) produces meaningful, robust strength and maintenance adaptations (~60–70% of maximal response), establishing the baseline for Minimum Effective Volume (MEV).

* **Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW.** *Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass: A systematic review and meta-analysis.* **J Sports Sci.** 2017 Jun;35(11):1073-1082. [PMID: 27433992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/)
  * *Key Finding:* A graded dose-response curve exists between weekly volume and hypertrophy: <5 sets/week (suboptimal), 5–9 sets/week (effective), and 10+ sets/week (optimal for trained cohorts), with diminishing returns past 20 sets.

## 4. Frequency and Inter-Set Rest Intervals

* **Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Krieger J.** *How many times per week should a muscle be trained to maximize muscle hypertrophy? A systematic review and meta-analysis of studies examining the effects of resistance training frequency.* **J Sports Sci.** 2019 Jun;37(11):1286-1295. [PMID: 30558493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/)
  * *Key Finding:* When total weekly volume is equated, training frequency (1x vs 2x vs 3x/week) does not significantly impact hypertrophic outcomes. Frequency serves as a logistical tool to distribute weekly sets so that no single session exceeds the per-session effective ceiling (~8–10 sets/muscle).

* **Schoenfeld BJ, Pope ZK, Benik FM, Hester GM, Sellers J, Nooner JL, Schnaiter JA, Bond-Williams KE, Carter AS, Ross CL, Just BL, Henselmans M, Krieger JW.** *Longer Interset Rest Periods Enhance Muscle Strength and Hypertrophy in Resistance-Trained Men.* **J Strength Cond Res.** 2016 Jul;30(7):1805-1812. [PMID: 26605807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/)
  * *Key Finding:* 3-minute rest intervals elicited significantly greater muscle thickness and 1RM gains than 1-minute intervals by maintaining mechanical tension and training volume across multiple sets.

## 5. Nutrition, Protein Ingestion Kinetics & Diet Breaks

* **Trommelen J, van Lieshout GAA, Nyakayiru J, Holwerda AM, Smeets JSJ, Hendriks FK, van Kranenburg JMX, Zorenc AH, Senden JM, Goessens JPB, Gijsen AP, van Loon LJC.** *The anabolic response to protein ingestion during recovery from exercise has no upper limit in magnitude and duration in vivo in humans.* **Cell Rep Med.** 2023 Dec 19;4(12):101324. [PMID: 38118410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38118410/)
  * *Key Finding:* Ingestion of 100 g protein post-exercise resulted in a sustained, prolonged anabolic response (>12 hours) with elevated myofibrillar protein synthesis rates and plasma amino acid availability, disproving the classic "30 g per meal ceiling" myth.

* **Peos JJ, Helms ER, Fournier PA, Ong J, Hall C, Krieger J, Sainsbury A.** *Continuous versus Intermittent Dieting for Fat Loss and Fat-Free Mass Retention in Resistance-trained Adults: The ICECAP Trial.* **Med Sci Sports Exerc.** 2021 Aug 1;53(8):1685-1695. [PMID: 33587549](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587549/)
  * *Key Finding:* Intermittent energy restriction with 1-week diet breaks achieved equivalent fat loss and fat-free mass retention to continuous restriction, but produced significantly lower hunger, increased satiety (Peptide YY), and superior psychological adherence.

* **Peos JJ, Helms ER, Fournier PA, Krieger J, Sainsbury A.** *A 1-week diet break improves muscle endurance during an intermittent dieting regime in adult athletes: A pre-specified secondary analysis of the ICECAP trial.* **PLoS One.** 2021 Feb 25;16(2):e0247292. [PMID: 33630880](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33630880/)
  * *Key Finding:* 1-week diet breaks in resistance-trained athletes significantly restored muscle endurance in the lower limbs (+15% total work output), restored resting energy expenditure (+200 kJ/day), and improved mental alertness.

* **Morton RW, Murphy KT, McKellar SR, Schoenfeld BJ, Henselmans M, Helms E, Aragon AA, Devries MC, Banfield L, Krieger JW, Phillips SM.** *A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength in healthy adults.* **Br J Sports Med.** 2018 Mar;52(6):376-384. [PMID: 28698222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)
  * *Key Finding:* Protein intakes up to 1.62 g/kg/day (with confidence intervals reaching ~2.2 g/kg/day) maximize fat-free mass accretion during resistance training. Intakes beyond this breakpoint provide minimal further hypertrophic benefit in energy balance or surplus.

* **Aragon AA, Schoenfeld BJ.** *Magnitude and composition of the energy surplus for maximizing muscle hypertrophy: Implications for bodybuilding and physique athletes.* **Strength & Conditioning Journal.** 2020;42(5):79-86.
  * *Key Finding:* Conservative energy surpluses (+200 to +350 kcal/day or ~0.25–0.5% body weight gain/week in trained lifters) optimize muscle protein synthesis while mitigating unnecessary adipose accrual.

* **Helms ER, Aragon AA, Fitschen PJ.** *Evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: nutrition and supplementation.* **J Int Soc Sports Nutr.** 2014 May 12;11:20. [PMID: 24864135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24864135/)
  * *Key Finding:* Established foundational rates for fat loss (0.5–1.0% body weight/week) and macronutrient distribution (1.8–2.7 g/kg protein in deficit) to preserve lean mass during hypocaloric phases.

## 6. Concurrent Training, Aerobic Exercise & The Interference Effect

* **Schumann M, Feuerbacher JF, Sünkeler M, Freitag N, Rønnestad BR, Doma K, Lundberg TR.** *Compatibility of Concurrent Aerobic and Strength Training for Skeletal Muscle Size and Function: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* **Sports Med.** 2022 Mar;52(3):601-612. [PMID: 34757594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/)
  * *Key Finding:* Meta-analysis of 43 studies demonstrating that concurrent aerobic and strength training does NOT compromise muscle hypertrophy ($p = 0.919$) or maximal strength ($p = 0.446$). Attenuation of explosive strength is mitigated when aerobic and lifting sessions are separated by $\ge 3$ hours.

* **Lundberg TR, Feuerbacher JF, Sünkeler M, Schumann M.** *The Effects of Concurrent Aerobic and Strength Training on Muscle Fiber Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* **Sports Med.** 2022 Oct;52(10):2391-2403. [PMID: 35476184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35476184/)
  * *Key Finding:* Investigated fiber-level hypertrophy. Aerobic training performed via cycling elicited zero interference effect on muscle fibers compared to strength training alone, while high-impact running showed selective minor interference only on Type I fibers.

## 7. Workload Monitoring, ACWR & Autoregulation

* **Gabbett TJ.** *The training-injury prevention paradox: should athletes be training smarter and harder?* **Br J Sports Med.** 2016 Mar;50(5):273-280. [PMID: 26758673](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26758673/)
  * *Key Finding:* Established the Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR). Ratios between 0.8 and 1.3 represent the "sweet spot" for progressive adaptation, while acute load spikes exceeding ACWR 1.5 exponentially increase injury risk.
