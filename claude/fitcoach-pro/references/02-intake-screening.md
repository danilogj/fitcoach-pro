# Intake and screening — before any prescription

> **Keywords:** intake, anamnesis, new client, screening, PAR-Q, red flag, contraindication, medical referral, training history, available equipment, schedule, injury, medication, goal.

Never build a program without this data. A plan built on assumption is guaranteed rework — and when the assumption is about health, it is risk.

---

## Block 1 — Safety screening (mandatory, always first)

Adapted from PAR-Q+. Ask all seven, without softening them:

1. Has a doctor ever said you have a heart condition or high blood pressure, or that you should only exercise under supervision?
2. Do you feel chest pain at rest or during exertion?
3. Have you lost balance from dizziness, or fainted, in the last 12 months?
4. Do you have a bone or joint problem that could be made worse by physical activity?
5. Are you on any ongoing medication? Which, and what for?
6. Are you pregnant, or have you given birth in the last 6 months?
7. Do you know of any other reason why you should not exercise?

### Acting on the result

| Answer | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| All "no" | Proceed to the full intake. |
| Any "yes" | **Do not prescribe yet.** Refer for medical clearance and help the trainer write a specific question for the physician: which movements, what intensity, what stop signals. |
| Chest pain, fainting, disproportionate shortness of breath | Stop immediately. Medical evaluation before any testing, including load testing. |

### Red flags that appear later, during coaching

Stop and refer if any of these show up: chest pain or pain radiating to arm or jaw, dizziness or syncope, disproportionate shortness of breath, acute joint pain that worsens with movement, numbness or loss of strength in a limb, low-back pain radiating down the leg, dark urine after intense training, unintentional weight loss, amenorrhea, signs of an eating disorder.

**The rule that is not negotiable:** diffuse pain in the muscle belly that improves as they warm up is delayed onset soreness — they train. Localized pain in a joint or tendon, sharp, worsening with movement — that pattern does not get trained. A skipped exercise costs nothing; an injury costs six weeks.

## Block 2 — The goal, and the goal behind it

Ask the goal, then ask the deadline and the success criterion. "Lose weight" with no number and no date is not a goal, it is a wish — and it makes it impossible to say whether the program worked.

Translate it into one of four operable targets:

| The client says | Operable goal | Calorie direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "Put on size", "get bigger" | Hypertrophy | Slight surplus |
| "Lose weight", "get lean", "tone up" | Fat loss with muscle retention | Moderate deficit |
| "Get strong", "lift more" | Strength | Maintenance or slight surplus |
| "Health", "stop being sedentary" | Conditioning and functional capacity | Maintenance |

**If the client wants both at once** — build muscle and lose fat — tell them the truth, split by profile: it happens meaningfully in beginners, in people returning after a long layoff, and in people with high body fat. In a trained client at low body fat, picking one direction per block delivers more. Do not sell recomposition as the default outcome.

## Block 3 — Training history

This sets the starting volume and the expected rate of progression.

- How long have you trained consistently, over your whole life?
- Have you trained in the last 3 months? How many times a week, actually?
- Current loads on the main patterns, if you know them (squat, bench, deadlift, pulldown).
- Have you been injured training? Where, when, did it resolve?
- What have you already tried that did not work — and why?

### Classification, and what it changes

| Profile | Working definition | Starting volume | Expected progression |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Beginner** | Under 6 months of consistent training | 8-12 sets/group/week | Load climbs almost weekly. Linear progression works. |
| **Intermediate** | 6 months to 2-3 years, technique settled | 12-18 sets/group/week | Load climbs every 2-4 weeks per exercise. Needs the double-progression criterion. |
| **Detrained intermediate** | Years of training, but stopped or at low volume for months | Starts like a beginner and climbs fast | Muscle memory: accelerated gains for the first 6-8 weeks. Do not calibrate expectations on that stretch. |
| **Advanced** | 3+ years, near their potential | 14-20 sets/group/week | Gains in months, not weeks. Needs deliberate periodization and variation. |

Record the profile in the intake — every other file references this classification.

## Block 4 — Real constraints

This is where most programs die.

**Schedule.** How many days per week, which days, how long per session including rest. Ask about the bad week, not the ideal one: "in a bad week, how many days actually happen?"

**Equipment.** Ask for the list of what exists at their gym, not the assumption. Confirm item by item: barbell and plates, dumbbells up to what weight and in what increments, flat and incline bench, squat rack or cage, high pulley, low pulley, adjustable dual cable, leg press, hack squat, leg extension, lying or seated leg curl, pec deck, reverse pec deck, lat pulldown, seated row, pull-up bar.

What is **not** there defines the program as much as what is. A plan built around a leg press at a gym with no leg press is a plan that does not get executed.

**Body.** Age, height, current weight, historical high and low, measurements if available.

**Routine.** Desk job or on their feet, daily steps if known, sleep in hours and consistency, stress level, alcohol.

**Food.** How many meals, who cooks, budget, allergies and intolerances, religious or ethical restrictions, what they hate eating, what they refuse to give up. An ignored meal plan is not a meal plan.

## Block 5 — Baseline

Without a baseline there is no demonstrable progress, and the client quits in month 3 convinced nothing changed.

Record on day one: weight (the average of the first 7 days, not a single reading), circumferences at waist, hip, arm, thigh and chest using the protocol in `06-body-assessment.md`, a standardized photo, starting loads on the main patterns, and the answer to "on a scale of 0 to 10, how would you rate your energy, your sleep and your drive today?"

That last line is worth more than it looks in the month-3 report.

---

## Asking for less

A full interview at every turn is why people abandon coaching tools. The screening block is mandatory and complete — everything after it degrades:

- **If a single field blocks the current answer, ask for that field.** "How many days a week can you train?" is a question. Re-running the whole intake to answer it is an interrogation.
- **Never re-ask what `intake.md` already records.** Read the file first.
- **Answer with what you have, and name the gap.** "Here is the block for 4 days with the equipment you listed. I assumed dumbbells go to at least 30 kg — if they stop at 20, the incline press needs a different progression."
- **Re-run the full intake only on a material change**: new goal, injury, gym change, schedule change, or a rate verdict that has been unsafe or stalled for two weeks.
